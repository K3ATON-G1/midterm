This is the midterm project from my graduate course in data visualization at
UMBC. For this project, I explored how childcare programs settings and 
reported opertaing schedules vary across the Zipcodes in the Baltimore area. 
Using the Maryland Department of Public Service (MDPS) dataset supplied by Emily 
DeMarines in 2026. The objective i set for myself while looking at the data was 
to create a useful comparison for Emily at district 9 of the data as well as 
make it readable for anyone interested un the local childcare access. 

My first exploration and visualization (EDA) focused on program capacity, age 
groups served, and the variation between zipcodes. While looking at the data it 
became cleare that a better analysis would be the settings as they relate to 
opertaing schedules. I had to use settings since the program Types are not 
singular and many have multiple types but all only have one setting. 

The paper sketchs and feedback form Emily on the Sketch portion helped move 
the project to the final version. I changed the original stacked bar chart into 
a mosaic to show both settings and zipcodes. Small sections were increased for 
readability. The weekly heatmap shows detailed operating schedules, while the 
line chart compares weekday and weekend averages. Feedback was also useful and
helped led me to revise colors, descriptions, and line patterns to make the 
comparison easier to follow. 

One important lesson for me was that percentages, missing information, and 
presentation are all impacted by the visual choices. When Emily looked at the 
data those were the main things she addressed. High percentages do not always 
represent many programs. This has something more to say on how i chose to 
visualize the data then it being an inherent issue but it was something i had 
to address. 

The project highlights how counts, percentages, and missing information can 
change hoe this data is presented. The data has a lot of missing schedules so 
they were treated as missing not closed. There is still so much that can be 
done while looking at this data. We have seen a small glimpse of the geographic
and time variations within childcare options for people. So now we can further 
examine whether care is affordable, vacancies in the centers, and if these 
programs are meeting family needs.
